AI in Dental Implant Dentistry: Identification, Planning and Prediction

AI has been studied for implant-system recognition, anatomical segmentation, planning support and outcome prediction, but evidence quality is not equally strong across those tasks.

In brief: 2026 umbrella reviews suggest stronger evidence for image-based identification and segmentation than for long-term prognosis. Biological variability and patient factors make prediction harder than recognition.

Four different AI tasks

Within AI in dentistry, implant applications include identifying an existing implant system, segmenting anatomy on CBCT, supporting implant planning and estimating risk or prognosis. These tasks require different evidence and should not be grouped under one generic “AI accuracy” claim.

Implant-system identification

Radiographic recognition can help when records for an existing implant are unavailable. Reviews report strong performance for identification tasks, but class coverage matters. A system trained on a limited set of brands needs an explicit unknown-class strategy rather than forced classification.

CBCT segmentation and planning

Automated segmentation can reduce preparation time and create a starting point for planning. Clinicians must be able to correct nerve-canal or bone boundaries because small segmentation errors may matter clinically.

Placement suggestions

Position and angulation depend on prosthetic planning, bone, anatomy, occlusion, soft tissue, aesthetics and surgical constraints. A model that sees only CBCT cannot represent all of that context. AI is better framed as a measurement or option-support layer.

Outcome prediction

Prediction is affected by smoking, systemic disease, bone quality, implant design, surgical technique, loading and follow-up. Retrospective models can learn associations, but they do not create causal certainty or guarantees.

Evaluation checklist

  1. Separate identification, segmentation, planning and prognosis.
  2. Check imaging modalities and scanners used in validation.
  3. Ask how unknown implant systems are handled.
  4. Require manual correction of segmentation and planning outputs.
  5. For prediction, demand external validation and calibration.
Evidence boundary

Recognition and segmentation are more constrained tasks; prognosis carries more biological uncertainty.
